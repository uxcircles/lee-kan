Our approach

We work within the estimated cost unless your matter turns out to be more complex, or it is difficult to know in advance what will be involved. In those cases we let you know in advance and agree an hourly rate with you.

VAT. All our fees, fixed or hourly, are subject to VAT at the current standard rate (20% at the time of publishing). Home Office fees, the Immigration Health Surcharge and HM Land Registry fees are not.

Fixed fee work is carried out or supervised by a Partner or Assistant Solicitor, and any work by a Trainee Solicitor or Paralegal is supervised. You can check each fee earner’s details on the SRA register, or ask us.

Where a fixed fee isn’t possible, we charge by the hour according to the qualification and experience of the person doing the work, and give you an estimate of the time involved first.

How long it takes

A sale typically completes in four to eight weeks, depending on the buyer’s searches and the co-operation of all parties. In a chain, dates have to be agreed by everyone.

What could change the cost?
  • the legal title is defective, or the title is unregistered
  • a management company must give a deed of covenant or consent
  • we need to comply with a restriction on the title
  • the correct consents were not obtained for alteration works
  • there is more than one mortgage secured on the property
  • you are buying or selling at auction, from a lender under a power of sale, or have tight deadlines
  • documents or information have to be requested more than once
  • the property is subject to a rent charge
  • you are receiving financial contributions from third parties, or using Help to Buy or a complex funding arrangement
  • the property is a new build or is being transferred out of an existing title
The purchase process, step by step
  1. Once we have the memorandum of sale, we check your identity and source of funds and take money on account for searches.
  2. The seller’s solicitors send the contract pack: title, plan, protocol forms, contract and EPC.
  3. We investigate title, order searches, approve the contract, draft the transfer and raise enquiries.
  4. With your mortgage offer, searches and replies in, we send you a written report on title.
  5. You sign the contract and transfer, by post or at our office.
  6. We exchange contracts. From this point the deal is binding.
  7. We draw down your mortgage, or ask for the balance needed to complete.
  8. On completion we send the funds and tell you and the agents so keys can be released.
  9. We file your SDLT return (or LTT in Wales) and register you as owner at HM Land Registry.
The sale process, step by step
  1. We obtain your title from HM Land Registry, or ask for your deeds if it is unregistered.
  2. We send you the property forms to complete.
  3. We prepare the contract pack for the buyer’s solicitors.
  4. We agree a completion date with you, the buyer and any related purchase.
  5. We exchange contracts. You are then committed to completing on the agreed date.
  6. On completion we pay off your mortgage and the estate agent, and send you the proceeds or use them for your purchase.
Exclusions

These prices are for individuals selling or buying their own home. Extra work is likely if the property is tenanted, you are acting as a company director, the property is being transferred out of an existing title, or you are selling as an executor or attorney.

Probate

Probate is the process of administering someone’s estate after they die: gathering their money, assets and possessions, paying taxes and debts, and passing on the inheritance. Where there is no Will, the estate is administered under a Grant of Administration and the rules of intestacy.

What we can do

  • Gather full details of the estate’s assets and debts, usually the longest stage
  • Complete the inheritance tax return and pay any tax due
  • Apply for the Grant of Probate or Grant of Administration
  • Repay the deceased’s outstanding debts
  • Advertise the Trustee Act notice
  • Distribute the estate according to the Will

All probate work is supervised by our senior partner, Dominic Lee. Most estates take about a year. International estates, and disputes between executors, beneficiaries, creditors or HMRC, can take longer.
